# Marketing Fundamentals — The Mark Ritson Philosophy

**Prepared:** 25 July 2026
**Subject:** A working summary of marketing fundamentals as taught by Mark Ritson
**Purpose:** A practical, one-document refresher on the principles, sequence and mindset Ritson champions — useful for planning, briefing agencies, and cutting through marketing hype.

> **About the source.** Mark Ritson is a marketing professor, *Marketing Week* columnist and creator of the "Mini MBA in Marketing." His philosophy is deliberately unglamorous: evidence over fashion, discipline over creativity-for-its-own-sake, and a strict sequence of diagnosis → strategy → tactics. He draws heavily on the empirical work of the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute (Byron Sharp) and the effectiveness research of Les Binet & Peter Field. This summary distils the recurring themes of his teaching; it is a synthesis, not a direct quotation.

---

## 1. The core worldview

A handful of convictions sit underneath everything Ritson teaches:

1. **Marketing isn't hard, but it is disciplined.** The fundamentals are well understood and evidence-based. Most failures come from skipping steps, not from a lack of cleverness.
2. **Diagnosis before strategy, strategy before tactics.** The single most common error is jumping straight to tactics (a TikTok campaign, a rebrand, a new logo) before understanding the market or deciding what you're trying to achieve. *"Tactics without strategy is the noise before defeat."*
3. **"Bothism" beats binary thinking.** Marketing is plagued by false either/or debates. The right answer is almost always *both*: brand **and** performance, long **and** short, emotional **and** rational, digital **and** traditional, acquisition **and** retention, art **and** science.
4. **Be evidence-led, not fad-led.** Be sceptical of the shiny new thing — the metaverse, NFTs, "authenticity," most brand-purpose posturing. Ask what the data actually shows about how brands grow.
5. **The market, not your opinion, is the authority.** Good marketers are market-oriented: they look outward at customers and competitors before looking inward at their own preferences.

---

## 2. The planning sequence (the Ritson "spine")

Ritson teaches marketing as an ordered process. Do these in order; don't skip forward.

| # | Stage | The question it answers |
|---|-------|-------------------------|
| 1 | **Market orientation / diagnosis** | What is really going on in this market? |
| 2 | **Research** | What do the numbers and the customers tell us? |
| 3 | **Segmentation** | How does the market divide into groups? |
| 4 | **Targeting** | Which groups will we serve — and which will we ignore? |
| 5 | **Positioning** | In the minds of those targets, what do we want to stand for? |
| 6 | **Objectives** | What, specifically, are we trying to achieve? |
| 7 | **Tactics (the marketing mix)** | How will we deliver it — product, price, place, promotion? |
| 8 | **Metrics & controls** | How will we know if it worked? |

Stages 3–5 (Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning) are the **strategic core**. Everything before them is diagnosis; everything after is execution.

---

## 3. Diagnosis and research

- **Start by understanding the market**, not by deciding what to do. Map the category, the competitors, the channels, the trends and the customers *before* forming a plan.
- **Use both quantitative and qualitative research.** Numbers tell you *what* is happening; talking to real customers tells you *why*. Neither alone is enough.
- **Don't outsource your thinking to the customer.** Customers are poor at predicting their own future behaviour and can't design your strategy for you. Research informs judgement; it doesn't replace it.
- **Spend real time with customers.** Ritson pushes marketers to get out of the office and observe/interview actual buyers — including the many "light" and non-buyers, not just loyal fans.

## 4. The strategic core — Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning (STP)

Ritson calls STP the "beating heart" of marketing strategy.

### 4.1 Segmentation
Divide the market into meaningful groups. Segments can be built on needs, behaviours, value, occasion or demographics — but they must be **distinct, sizeable and reachable**. Segmentation is a *map of the whole market*, not just the bit you like.

### 4.2 Targeting
Choose which segments to pursue **and, just as importantly, which to ignore.** Key Ritson points:
- **You cannot be all things to all people.** Focus is a strategic act.
- **But don't over-target.** A common modern mistake is slicing the market so thin that you cut yourself off from growth. Targeting a *broad* set of category buyers is usually right (see §5).
- Targeting is a choice about *where you compete*, made with clear eyes about size and profitability.

### 4.3 Positioning
Decide what you want to own in the target's mind. Practical tools:
- **Points of difference (PODs)** — what makes you meaningfully different.
- **Points of parity (POPs)** — the table-stakes you must credibly match to be considered at all.
- Positioning must be **focused, credible and durable** — usually one or two ideas, not ten. If you claim everything, you own nothing.

## 5. How brands actually grow (the Ehrenberg-Bass evidence)

Ritson leans heavily on Byron Sharp's *How Brands Grow*. The empirical laws:

- **Penetration beats loyalty.** Brands grow mainly by acquiring *more buyers* — especially light and occasional buyers — not by squeezing more loyalty out of existing heavy buyers.
- **Reach is king.** Advertising and distribution should aim to reach *all category buyers*, not a narrow niche.
- **Double jeopardy.** Small brands suffer twice: they have fewer buyers *and* those buyers are slightly less loyal. The route out is growing penetration.
- **Mental availability + physical availability.** Growth = being easy to *think of* (mental availability, built by advertising and distinctive assets) **and** easy to *buy* (physical/distribution availability). Both must be built.
- **Distinctive brand assets.** Colours, logos, characters, sounds, taglines — the assets that make a brand instantly recognisable. Build them, protect them, use them consistently.
- **Category Entry Points (CEPs).** Link the brand to the situations and cues that trigger category purchase, so you come to mind at the buying moment.
- **Distinctiveness vs differentiation.** Sharp argues distinctiveness (being recognisable) matters more than differentiation (being different). Ritson's nuance: pursue **both** — be distinctive *and* meaningfully positioned.

## 6. Brand-building and advertising effectiveness (Binet & Field)

From Les Binet & Peter Field's *The Long and the Short of It*:

- **The 60/40 rule.** As a long-run guide, split budget roughly **60% brand building / 40% sales activation.** Brand building compounds; activation converts demand that brand building created.
- **Long and short do different jobs:**
  - *Brand building* — broad reach, emotional, long-term, grows the base and pricing power. Effects are slow but cumulative.
  - *Sales activation* — tightly targeted, rational, short-term, harvests existing demand. Effects are sharp but decay quickly.
- **Emotion out-performs rational persuasion for long-term growth.** Campaigns that make people *feel* something build stronger memory and future sales than purely informational ones.
- **Fame, feeling and fluency** are the qualities of effective advertising: being talked about, generating emotion, and being unmistakably *you*.
- **Excess Share of Voice (ESOV).** If your **share of voice** exceeds your **share of market**, you tend to gain share. Under-spend relative to your size and you tend to shrink.

## 7. The marketing mix — tactics done last, not first

Only once diagnosis, STP and objectives are set do you build the mix. The classic **4 Ps** (extended to **7 Ps** for services):

- **Product** — the offer, features, quality, range, service.
- **Price** — arguably the **most powerful and most neglected profit lever.** Small pricing changes move profit far more than volume does; brand building earns the right to charge more. Don't default to discounting.
- **Place** — distribution and availability; being where and when customers buy (physical availability).
- **Promotion** — advertising, media, comms — the part marketers over-focus on.
- *(Services add)* **People, Process, Physical evidence.**

The mix must be **internally consistent** and, above all, faithful to the positioning. Every P should reinforce the same strategic idea.

---

## 8. Objectives, metrics and money

- **Separate the layers of objectives:** *business* objectives (revenue, profit, share) → *marketing* objectives (penetration, awareness, consideration) → *communications* objectives (reach, recall, attribution). Don't ask an ad to deliver a business outcome it can't own alone.
- **Make objectives SMART** — specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound.
- **Measure both the short and the long.** Judging brand-building work on this quarter's sales is a category error; it does its work over years.
- **Marketing is accountable.** Tie activity back to commercial outcomes, and be honest about what did and didn't work.

---

## 9. Ritson's recurring provocations

Things he is well known for arguing — useful as sanity checks:

- **Most "brand purpose" is nonsense.** For the vast majority of brands, an invented higher social "purpose" is neither believable nor commercially useful. Serve customers well; don't cosplay as a social movement.
- **Digital is a channel, not a religion.** Online is essential *and* not automatically superior. Beware marketers who abandon proven principles because a medium is new.
- **Chase effectiveness, not awards or fashion.** Novelty and creativity are means, not ends.
- **Target broad, not narrow.** Over-segmentation and hyper-personalisation often cap growth.
- **Respect the classics.** Kotler, Ehrenberg-Bass, Binet & Field — the evidence base is decades deep. Learn it before "disrupting" it.

## 10. One-page checklist

Before signing off any plan, ask:

- [ ] Have we **diagnosed** the market before deciding tactics?
- [ ] Do we have **both** quantitative data and real qualitative customer contact?
- [ ] Is our **segmentation** a map of the whole market?
- [ ] Have we made a genuine **targeting** choice — including what to ignore — without over-narrowing?
- [ ] Is our **positioning** focused, credible and durable (clear PODs and POPs)?
- [ ] Are we building **mental *and* physical availability**?
- [ ] Are we investing in **distinctive brand assets** and using them consistently?
- [ ] Is the budget roughly **60/40 brand/activation**, with **emotional** brand work?
- [ ] Is our **share of voice** ahead of our share of market (positive ESOV)?
- [ ] Have we set **layered, SMART objectives** and a way to measure long *and* short?
- [ ] Is the whole **mix (7 Ps)** consistent with the positioning — and is **price** treated as a lever, not an afterthought?
- [ ] Have we chosen **evidence over fashion** at every step?
