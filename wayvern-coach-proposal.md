## 0. The blunt version (read this even if you read nothing else)

If your pitch is *"I build websites,"* Squarespace and Wix already beat you — on price, speed, and
now AI. You will lose that fight. **So don't have it.**

You win by refusing to sell what they sell. They sell a *tool*. You sell an *outcome and a
relationship*: more of the right customers, time given back to the owner, and one accountable human
who answers the phone. A builder can't do those three things. That gap is your entire business.

The rest of this doc is how to make that real — and where you should honestly concede.

---

## 1. Reframing the question

You asked: *"Why would a small company choose me over Squarespace/Wix?"*

A coach's job is to challenge the question before answering it. The honest reframe:

- **Wrong question:** "Why pick me instead of a $20/month builder?" — that invites a price war you lose.
- **Right question:** *"For which customer is a $20 builder the wrong answer — and am I unmistakably the right one for them?"*

Most small businesses **should** use Squarespace/Wix, and you should be relaxed about saying so. Your
market is the slice for whom DIY is a false economy. Define that slice precisely or you're just an
expensive version of a cheaper thing.

---

## 2. The honest competitive reality

Know the opponent before you swing.

| Dimension | Squarespace / Wix | A small agency (you) |
|---|---|---|
| Price | ~$16–65/mo, fixed, transparent | $1.5k–8k+ build, then retainer — 50–200× their monthly |
| Speed to live | Hours to days | Weeks |
| Brand trust | Global, "safe choice" | Unknown — you must earn it every time |
| AI build (2026) | Describe-it-and-it-builds; eating the bottom of the market fast | Only wins if you're *above* what AI produces |
| What they actually deliver | A tool that *can* make a site | A site is the floor, not the product |
| What they **can't** do | Strategy, accountability, local nuance, "do it for me," outcomes | This is your whole moat |

**The threat you must internalise:** AI builders are collapsing the price and skill floor of "make me
a website." The phrase *"I can build you a site"* is worth less every quarter. If that's your value
prop, you're standing on melting ice. Everything below is about moving up the value chain to where AI
and templates can't follow: **judgement, accountability, and results.**

---

## 3. Where you win — the seven levers a builder can't pull

These are the real answers to "why choose me." Pick the 2–3 that are *true for you* and own them.

1. **Time, not software.** The owner's hours are the scarcest resource in a small business and worth
   more spent on their trade than fighting a page editor. You sell *"never think about your website
   again."* Done-for-you, done-right, done-once.
2. **Outcomes, not pages.** Squarespace hands over an empty template; it never tells you what to say,
   who to target, or how to turn a visitor into a booking. You sell **leads, bookings, revenue** —
   the site is just the machine that produces them.
3. **Strategy and judgement.** Positioning, message, offer, who to ignore. A template has no opinion.
   You do. This is the part AI imitates worst because it requires understanding *this* business.
4. **The whole growth system, integrated.** Google Business Profile, local SEO, reviews, booking,
   payments, CRM/email follow-up, tracking. Builders give you a box; you wire up the plumbing that
   makes phones ring.
5. **One accountable human, locally.** A real person in NZ/AU who picks up the phone, knows the local
   market, and *owns the result*. When their site breaks before a big weekend, they call you — not a
   support queue in another timezone.
6. **Conversion-grade craft.** The difference between a site that looks fine and one that *sells*.
   Most DIY sites quietly leak customers. You can prove the lift.
7. **Trust by association + speed of help.** You carry credibility (case studies, niche reputation)
   and you move when it matters. That's worth a premium to someone whose livelihood rides on it.

---

## 4. Where you lose — concede these on purpose

A coach won't let you pretend you're good at everything. **Walk away from these fights:**

- **Price-shoppers.** Anyone whose first question is "how cheap?" is a Squarespace customer. Bless
  them and let them go.
- **Hobby / pre-revenue / "just need *something* up."** DIY is genuinely the right call. Refer them.
- **People who want full self-service control** and enjoy tinkering. Not your buyer.
- **Pure commodity "make it look like the template" jobs** with no strategy attached — that's exactly
  the work AI builders are eating. Don't anchor your business there.

Conceding these makes your "why me" sharper, not weaker. A positioning that's right for everyone is
right for no one.

---

## 5. The positioning that makes the choice obvious

Three moves turn "another web guy" into the obvious pick:

1. **Niche down hard.** "Web design" competes with the planet. *"Websites that book out NZ trades /
   fill clinics / fill restaurant tables"* competes with almost no one, and lets you speak the
   customer's language, reuse what works, and charge for expertise. (Note the Northbound IP doc: the
   name isn't globally unique and the key domains are taken offshore — a tight niche helps the brand
   stand out where the name alone won't.)
2. **Productise the offer.** Not "hourly web work" (sounds like a cost) but a named package with a
   promise and a price — e.g. *"The [Trade] Growth Site: live in 21 days, built to get you booked,
   $X + $Y/mo to keep it ranking and running."* Removes the apples-to-oranges price comparison with a
   builder.
3. **Sell the retainer, not the build.** The build is the doorway; the relationship is the business.
   Ongoing care, updates, SEO, reporting = recurring revenue for you and "I never have to worry about
   it" for them. This is precisely what a builder *structurally cannot* sell.

---

## 6. Your "why choose me" answer — steal this

When a small business owner asks *"Why you and not Squarespace?"*, don't defend. Reframe:

> *"Honestly? If you've got the time and you enjoy building it yourself, Squarespace is great — use
> it. Most of my clients tried that and found the site quietly sat there not bringing in work, while
> the build ate their weekends. They don't hire me for a website — they've usually already got one.
> They hire me to get them **booked**, and to **never think about it again**. I build it, I make sure
> the right people find it, and when something needs doing you call me, not a help desk overseas. The
> site's the easy part — getting you customers and your time back is the job."*

That answer (a) doesn't trash the competitor, (b) disqualifies the wrong buyer, (c) moves the contest
off price and onto outcomes + time + accountability — the three things a tool can't sell.

## 7. Coach's verdict & scorecard

| Test | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Can you beat Squarespace/Wix on price? | **No — don't try.** |
| Can you beat them on "I'll do it all for you, and own the result"? | **Yes — easily. They can't follow.** |
| Can you beat them on outcomes (leads/revenue) vs. a tool? | **Yes — if you can prove it.** |
| Is "I build websites" a durable position in 2026? | **No.** AI is eating it. Move up the value chain. |
| Do you currently have a niche + productised offer + retainer? | **Unknown — this is your homework.** |

**Bottom line:** There's a strong, defensible answer to "why choose me" — but it lives in *time,
outcomes, and accountability*, never in *price or the website itself*. If your proposal leads with
"great websites," it's aimed at the one fight you can't win. Re-aim it.
